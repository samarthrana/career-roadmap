Proof first. Apply early. Three lanes.

The June roadmap was learn-then-apply over 26 weeks. The research says that misses the window: with a typical notice period, first interviews must start by late January. So this plan builds proof in December, starts reaching people this week, and runs interview practice every week until an offer is signed.

When phases overlap, what gives way. The 12 hours is a hard cap. Order of protection: applications → interview drills → flagship → SQL extras. W5–W6: flagship 4 h (finish only, no new features) · applications 4 · drills 2 · SQL 2. A week with 2+ live loops: drills 0, applications 1.

Take-homes — what fails them

From a hiring manager who reviews them (Aakash Gupta with Sumant Malhotra):

  • An AI feature with no eval method, no cost model and no success metric. "That 20% is evals and the economics" — the part most candidates miss. Your flagship is the template.
  • Building something nobody asked for, or demonstrating everything they didn't ask for in the same answer. Pick one problem and say what you cut.
  • Ignoring the brief's limits — e.g. a prompt that says "no more than two pages".
  • Work you can't defend live. A deep-dive round follows: why this product, why this feature first, which metrics and why.

The money conversation

  • Only answer "expected?" with a prepared anchor / target / floor — they open at the bottom of whatever range you give.
  • Always say "fixed" — otherwise they hear CTC.
  • Never give a range whose top is your floor.
  • Title over first salary — the first PM title unlocks the next jump.
  • Ask about a notice buyout — product companies often reimburse it.

Why "no solutions until it localises" works

The mechanism: every cause has a reach. A broken payment gateway hits only payers; an Android release hits only Android. So when you cut the drop by platform, region, user type, and step, the place where the drop stops being everywhere is the cause's fingerprint. Solutions proposed before that point are guesses — and interviewers are grading how you find the cause, not how fast you name a fix.

When it returns nothing — the drop is equally everywhere — that itself is the answer: look for something with total reach: tracking broke, a holiday, a sitewide outage, a definition change. The analogy: a doctor pressing different parts of your stomach. Where it hurts tells them more than any theory.

Business model of the week — the template

Five lines, under two minutes: who pays · for what · how often · the unit-economics line (what one user is worth vs what one costs) · one risk. Then one stretch question: "why is it worth ₹X?" Example: Reddit — about 95% of revenue is advertising ($847M of $895M in Q3 2026); the rest is "other revenue". One risk to name: if AI answers replace search, fewer readers arrive from Google.

The skills — to what bar, from where

Where to learn each one: the Learning library below. Every skill has a pass/fail bar. If you can't pass it, you don't have it yet — no matter how many videos you've watched. All resources are free.

1 · SQL — 59% of postings

BAR30-day retention and funnel drop-off, with CTEs and window functions, each in under 20 minutes.

2 · Evals — 23% of postings

The mechanism: a normal test checks that 2+2 always gives 4. An AI gives a slightly different answer every time, so you can't test one answer — you test a set of realistic questions and count how often it's right, and how it's wrong. The counting by type is the important part: "12 of 40 failed" tells you nothing; "7 failed because it summed a column that should be averaged" tells you exactly what to fix. The analogy: a teacher who marks a mock exam by mistake type, not by score.

BARThe flagship's 40-question eval set, an error analysis by failure type, a ship threshold, and a before/after re-run.

3 · Agents and the AI vocabulary — 59%

BARExplain your own Claude work using agents, tools, context, skills, MCP and cost per task — without notes, in under two minutes.

4 · Prototyping in an interview — 45%, your edge

They don't grade the code. They grade judgment while building: deciding what to build before touching the tool, pushing back on the AI's first output, naming edge cases. Failure: jumping straight into building, and accepting the first answer.

BARA 30-minute build, narrated aloud, where you reject or change the AI's first output at least once and say why.

5 · Experimentation — 32%

The mechanism: show the change to half the users and compare. The trap is noise — small differences happen by chance. Smaller effects need far more users to see — and it's squared: an effect 10× smaller needs roughly 100× the users. That's why a low-traffic page can't test a tiny tweak. That's why you always say up front what lift you're looking for, and add a guardrail metric that must not get worse.

BAROne experiment written for each work sample: hypothesis · primary metric · guardrail · rough sample-size reasoning · kill line.

Confidence is produced, not waited for

In past final rounds the knowledge was there. What was missing was answers rehearsed until they came out decision-first under a poker face.

  • Every story rehearsed without notes until the first sentence is always the decision.
  • Every number passes the five-second test — if you can't say where it came from, it's cut.
  • An interview log after every round, same day: what they asked · what I said · what I'd say now. Now standard after every round.
  • One mock a month with a real PM — the only way to practise against someone who isn't on your side.
  • The scoreboard shows progress on the weeks that feel like nothing is moving.
  • Silence and poker faces are style, not verdict. Read behaviour — what they share, what they ask about next — not warmth.
