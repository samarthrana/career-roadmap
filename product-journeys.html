How to use this in 20 minutes a day

  • Read one journey a day, open, all the way through. Then close it and say the lesson out loud in two sentences. If you can't, read the "why" again.
  • Read by lens, not by company. Pick a lens (say, unit economics), read every journey tagged with it. The pattern sticks better than any one story.
  • Drill mode (Catalogue tab) hides everything but the name. Say the lesson, then reveal the interview line to check.
  • Before an interview, open the Company map for that company's lane and re-read those 4–6 journeys.
  • Numbers: only say a number from a fact with a source link, and say "as of" the date. Facts on the Additional tab are not for quoting.
  • Make it yours. The interview line is a starting point. The version that lands is the one connected to something you've built or seen.

Telling a story without sounding rehearsed

Interviewers don't want the Wikipedia version. They want to see how you think. Five beats, about 60 seconds:

  • 1. What I noticed — the observation, ideally from using the product. "Paying a chaiwala by UPI is faster than finding change."
  • 2. The decision they made — the bet, stated as a choice with an alternative. "UPI was made free for merchants and works across every bank and app, instead of each wallet being its own island."
  • 3. Why it worked (or didn't) — the mechanism. "Free removed every merchant's reason to say no, and the network effect sat in the rail, not one app."
  • 4. What it changed in how I think — the lesson in your words. "Free gets you scale, but someone pays the bill, so decide early who."
  • 5. How I'd use it here — connect to their product. "For your app I'd ask which part of the flow users would pay for, before adding more free features."

Traps

  • Never trash the founder or the company — someone in the room may know them. Say what the evidence shows, not who was foolish.
  • Five-second numbers only. If you can't say where a number came from, drop it.
  • Date your facts. "As of November 2026" — status changes fast (Paytm, Zepto and Canva all changed this year).
  • Don't stack stories. One story told well beats three name-dropped.
  • Have a counter-view ready. "The case against this is…" is what makes it sound like thinking, not memorising.
